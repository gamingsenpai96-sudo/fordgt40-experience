THE ARCHITECT OF REVENGE

HENRY FORD II ("THE DEUCE")

HENRY FORD II • CEO & PRESIDENT

"You better win."

Henry Ford II handing Carroll Shelby his personal calling card before Le Mans 1966

In May 1963, Ford executives spent several weeks in Maranello auditing Ferrari’s books to finalize an $18 million buyout. At the eleventh hour, Enzo Ferrari read the contract clause granting Ford corporate veto over Ferrari’s racing division budget. Enzo cursed the American executives in Bolognese dialect, terminated negotiations, and threw Ford out into the street.

Furious, Henry Ford II summoned his top executives to Dearborn and formed the High-Performance Committee with one directive: "Spend whatever it takes. Win Le Mans." On June 19, 1966, Ford personally attended the finish as three GT40 Mk IIs swept the podium in a staged formation.

PROGRAMME CHIEF & RACING ICON

CARROLL SHELBY

CARROLL SHELBY • SHELBY AMERICAN

"Yesterday's history, tomorrow's a mystery, so let's go like hell today."

Carroll Shelby • Shelby American

After the initial 1964 GT40 campaign ended in catastrophic mechanical DNFs under John Wyer, Ford delivered the entire racing programme to Carroll Shelby’s hangar at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). Shelby immediately diagnosed the car's primary flaws: aerodynamic front lift, inadequate cooling, and an overburdened small-block motor.

Shelby replaced the small-block engine with the massive 427 cu in (7.0-liter) NASCAR V8, reshaped the front radiator ducting, and proved the concept by taking victory at the 1965 Daytona 2000 km and the 1966 12 Hours of Sebring.

ENGINEER, RACER & HERO

KEN MILES

KEN MILES • CHIEF DEVELOPMENT DRIVER

"There's a point at 7,000 RPM where everything fades. The machine becomes weightless. Just disappears. And all that's left is a body moving through space and time."

Ken Miles • Chief Development Driver

Ken Miles was the soul of the GT40 development. A former British tank mechanic and brilliant intuitive engineer, Miles spent hundreds of grueling test hours around Riverside Raceway in California, pushing prototypes until components fractured so they could be redesigned before France.

In the 1966 race, Miles and co-driver Denny Hulme shattered the Le Mans lap record. On Sunday morning, Miles held a commandingly comfortable lead. He was instructed by Leo Beebe to slow down to allow Bruce McLaren's #2 car and Ronnie Bucknum's #5 car to catch up for Ford's corporate publicity photo. When the flag waved, Le Mans officials awarded victory to McLaren because his car started 20 meters further back on the grid, cruelly robbing Miles of the historic Triple Crown (Daytona, Sebring, and Le Mans in a single calendar year).

IL COMMENDATORE

ENZO FERRARI

ENZO FERRARI • IL COMMENDATORE

"I have never seen anyone win a race by saving fuel or saving brakes. The client is not always right. My cars are not commodities."

Enzo Ferrari • Maranello

Enzo Ferrari regarded production sports cars merely as necessary nuisances sold to wealthy aristocrats to pay for racing tires, fuel, and engine blocks. When Ford's corporate attorneys presented a 200-page buyout contract that restricted Scuderia Ferrari's racing independence, Enzo famously remarked that no committee in Detroit would ever dictate which races Ferrari entered.

He encircled the clause in purple ink, wrote "Non ci siamo" ("We are not agreed"), walked out of the conference room, and had lunch at the Ristorante Cavallino.

CHIEF TECHNICAL DIRECTOR

MAURO FORGHIERI

MAURO FORGHIERI • TECHNICAL DIRECTOR

"Racing is a great laboratory. Every nut, every bolt must justify its weight in speed."

Mauro Forghieri • Scuderia Ferrari

Appointed by Enzo Ferrari at just 27 years old, Mauro Forghieri restructured Ferrari's technical department. For 1966, Forghieri replaced the spaceframe architecture with a semi-monocoque chassis featuring aero-stressed duralumin skins bonded to steel tubes, reducing chassis weight to barely 1,587 lbs (720 kg) — over 1,000 lbs lighter than the heavy Ford GT40!

Forghieri also introduced Lucas indirect mechanical fuel injection and twin overhead camshafts per cylinder bank, allowing the 4.0-liter V12 to rev past 8,200 rpm with razor-sharp throttle response.

THE ITALIAN HERO

LORENZO BANDINI

LORENZO BANDINI • #20 FERRARI 330 P3

"When you drive for Ferrari, you drive for all of Italy. Every corner carries the heartbeat of millions."

Lorenzo Bandini • #20 Ferrari 330 P3

Lorenzo Bandini was beloved across Italy for his charm, courage, and blistering raw pace. Paired with 1964 Le Mans winner Jean Guichet in car #20, Bandini took the fight straight to Ford in the opening laps, trading the lead with Ken Miles as torrential rain swept the Sarthe circuit.

The blistering pace set by Bandini forced Ford to run flat-out, eventually causing gearbox and head gasket failures across multiple Ford customer entries before water pump and transmission gremlins struck the factory Ferrari entries in the dead of night.
